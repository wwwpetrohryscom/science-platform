Every measurement in [classical mechanics](/en/physics/mechanics-waves/classical-mechanics-explained) and everywhere else is a comparison against a unit, and until recently some of those units were objects. Since May 2019 none of them are. The whole system now rests on seven constants whose numerical values are fixed by definition, and understanding what that means is the difference between reading a measurement and taking one on trust.

## The seven

NIST lists them with their exact values and what each defines.

**ΔνCs**, the caesium-133 hyperfine transition frequency, "equal to 9,192,631,770 hertz", helping define "the second, meter, kilogram, and ampere".

**c**, the speed of light, "equal to 299,792,458 meters per second", helping define "the meter, kilogram, and kelvin".

**h**, the [Planck constant](/en/physics/mechanics-waves/what-changed-when-the-kilogram-changed), "equal to exactly 6.626 070 15 × 10⁻34 Joule seconds", helping define "the kilogram, kelvin, and candela".

**e**, the elementary charge, "equal to 1.602176634 × 10⁻19 coulombs", defining the ampere.

**k**, the Boltzmann constant, "equals 1.380649 × 10⁻23 joules/kelvin", helping define the kelvin.

**N_A**, the [Avogadro constant](/en/physics/matter-radiation/counting-atoms-and-the-mole), "equals 6.02214076 x 10²³ particles per mole", helping define the mole.

**K_cd**, luminous efficacy, "equal to 683 lumens per watt", helping define the candela.

## What "exact" means here

These values have no uncertainty, and that is a statement about the definition rather than about how well anything is known.

Before the redefinition, the kilogram was a metal cylinder and the Planck constant was measured against it, with an uncertainty. After it, the Planck constant is exact and the mass of that cylinder is measured against the definition, with an uncertainty. The total quantity of ignorance in the world is unchanged; it has been moved from the constant to the artefact.

This is the single most useful thing to understand about the modern SI. Fixing a constant does not make nature more precisely known. It relocates the uncertainty onto quantities that can be re-measured indefinitely, and away from a definition that could not be improved without redefining the unit.

## The definitions this produces

The base units are then written as consequences. The second "is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the cesium frequency ∆νCs… to be 9,192,631,770 when expressed in the unit Hz". The metre by taking c "to be 299,792,458 when expressed in the unit m s−1". The kilogram by taking h "to be 6.62607015 ×10−34 when expressed in the unit J s". The ampere by taking e "to be 1.602176634 × 10−19 when expressed in the unit C". The kelvin by taking k "to be 1.380 649 ×10−23 when expressed in the unit J K−1". The mole by the statement that it "contains exactly 6.02214076 × 1023 elementary entities". The candela by taking K_cd "to be 683 when expressed in the unit lm W−1".

Two features of that list are worth noticing.

**The definitions are nested.** The metre depends on the second, the kilogram on both, the ampere on the second and the elementary charge. A unit is not an independent object but a position in a dependency graph, which is why an improvement in timekeeping propagates outward into several other units.

**None of them prescribes an experiment.** The definition says what the unit is; realising it — building an apparatus that delivers a kilogram or an ampere to a stated uncertainty — is a separate activity, and several different realisations may compete. That separation is what allows a definition to outlive the technology that first made it practical.

## Why this is the foundation of everything else

The [traceability chain](/en/physics/mechanics-waves/calibration-and-traceability) that makes two laboratories' results comparable terminates here. Without a definition independent of any object, the top of the chain would be a thing that can be dropped, contaminated or lost — and for the kilogram, for 130 years, it was.

It also means the uncertainty in a good measurement is entirely in the realisation and the comparison, never in the definition. A statement about how well the second is known is a statement about clocks, not about the number 9,192,631,770.
