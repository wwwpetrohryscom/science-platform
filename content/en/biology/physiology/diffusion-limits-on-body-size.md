Diffusion has no speed. It has a relationship between distance and time in which the time goes as the square of the distance, which means it is extremely fast over a micron and hopeless over a centimetre. Everything about the architecture of large organisms follows from that asymmetry, and [physiology](/en/biology/physiology/physiology-explained) is in large part the study of the workarounds.

## The numbers

The NIH's *Regulation of Tissue Oxygenation* states the constraint directly: "diffusion puts an upper limit on the size of cells in regard to their need for oxygen", with a critical radius of "about 1 mm."

It also gives the two working distances that matter. "Diffusion is an efficient transport process over short distances (<100 μm)", and for a tissue supplied by blood "the circulating blood must be brought close to the cells (<10 μm)."

Those three numbers — one millimetre, one hundred microns, ten microns — are the whole argument. A spherical organism a millimetre in radius can supply its interior by diffusion from the surface. Anything larger cannot, and the interior becomes anoxic before it becomes distant in any everyday sense.

## What circulation actually does

The chapter states the solution in the terms that make it clear circulation is not a delivery system in the ordinary sense: the body uses "bulk flow (convection) to reduce the effective distance between the pumping action of the heart and the various parts of an organism", after which "nutrient and metabolic waste exchange takes place by passive diffusion, a transport mechanism which is most efficient over short distances."

Blood does not deliver oxygen to cells. It delivers oxygen to within ten microns of cells, and diffusion does the last step. The circulatory system is a distance-reduction device, and every feature of its anatomy — the branching down to capillaries, the enormous total capillary surface area, the fact that no cell in a mammal is far from one — is in service of that single job.

Read this way, [the heart and its circuit](/en/biology/physiology/circulation-and-the-heart) is a solution to a transport-geometry problem rather than a pump attached to an animal.

## The evolutionary consequence

The limit bites hardest before circulation exists, and it interacts with how much oxygen is available.

A 2021 study in *Nature Communications* on the oxygen suppression of macroscopic multicellularity makes the general point that "the ability for oxygen to diffuse into an organism places limits on organism size", and demonstrates it experimentally in clusters of yeast, where "only cells near the surface were capable of respiration, while the rest of the cluster interior is effectively anaerobic."

The study's more surprising result is that the relationship is not monotonic. Its model finds that "oxygen suppression of large size occurs between 0.0001 and 1% pO₂ PAL" — that is, over a specific intermediate range of atmospheric oxygen rather than simply at low levels. Below that range organisms are not aerobic anyway and the constraint does not apply; above it, diffusion delivers enough. In between, being large is penalised precisely because aerobic metabolism has become worth having and cannot be supplied to the interior.

That is a more interesting claim than "oxygen enabled large size", and it shows the shape of a real physical constraint: it does not simply scale with the resource, it has a window.

## What the limit does not say

**It is not a limit on cell size in general.** Some single cells are enormous — several centimetres in the case of certain algae — and they manage it by having a low [metabolic rate](/en/biology/physiology/metabolic-scaling-and-body-size), a large vacuole displacing metabolically active volume, or a flattened geometry that keeps every part near a surface. The constraint is on distance from an oxygen source, not on volume.

**It applies to oxygen more sharply than to other solutes.** Oxygen has low solubility in water and is consumed continuously, so the gradient it must maintain is steep. Nutrients that are stored, or consumed slowly, tolerate longer diffusion paths.

**It is a constraint on architecture, not a prohibition on size.** Insects reach centimetre scale without a circulatory oxygen carrier by using tracheae — air-filled tubes that carry gas most of the way and leave diffusion only the last short step. Same physics, different engineering, and it is the reason [gas exchange](/en/biology/physiology/respiration-and-gas-exchange) takes structurally different forms in different lineages.
